## Software investigation

The FX candidate precomputes fixed fold mix, chorus mix/depth and closed-gate target through the existing revision-aware coefficient cache. It preserves float operation order where values still vary, and preserves effect state and worker recovery. The attack-span and scheduler paths retain the v0.1.20 implementation. Deterministic WAVs were byte-identical to v0.1.20 for the 137 BPM modulated heavy-FX and 160 BPM GATE cases in both synchronous worker and single-core modes. The recovery suite and 16-voice modulated recording/replay passed. A native engineering build passed structural audits and package regeneration. Its application uses 162,804 bytes, static RAM 56,920/98,304 bytes and pool 312,420/344,064 bytes.

Five paired trials. Previous v0.1.20 worker mean and P99 increased 9.9% and 12.0% in the original run. Here the mean increased 1.0% while P99 improved 0.6%. The large v0.1.20 host regression did not reproduce. The native observation establishes zero reported misses for its own workload only.

In three paired steady-playback runs with sine LFO enabled, the coefficient candidate's worker mean decreased from 108.90 to 104.68 µs, but P99 rose from 260.35 to 298.86 µs. Its single-core mean rose from 104.78 to 109.29 µs and P99 from 286.20 to 333.44 µs. The candidate's P99 was higher in both host modes in both series. With uncertain native benefit and repeatable direction of the host tails, the coefficient experiment was reverted from the development tip. Retain the focused benchmark controls and the v0.1.20 DSP. No v0.1.21 release is warranted by this comparison.

Reproduction: use `device/tests/compare-dsp-timing.py` with the two revisions, `--chords --cpu-time --workload wet`; use `--full --runs 5` for the original fixture or `--modulated --runs 3` for steady modulated playback.
